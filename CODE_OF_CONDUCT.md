# Contributor Covenant — Code of Conduct

**Version 1.0, adapted from the Contributor Covenant 2.1 (CC-BY-4.0).**
The section structure is deliberately the standard one, so a reader who knows any
other project's code of conduct knows where to look for enforcement. If the
maintainers would rather adopt the canonical text word for word, that is a
one-commit change and the attribution line above is all the Covenant requires.

## Our commitment

We want the people who build, use, attack and defend SWP-1 to be able to do it
without being insulted, ignored, or made to defend their right to be here. This
project is technical, its subject matter is adversarial, and both of those things
work better when the people doing them are treated as colleagues.

We are committed to a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age,
body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, experience level, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or
sexual identity and orientation.

## Standards that matter here specifically

**Be good.** Use of welcoming, inclusive language; patience with somebody seeing
the protocol for the first time; graceful acceptance of constructive criticism;
focus on what is best for the project; empathy toward people whose use of this tool
is nothing like yours.

**Three failure modes this project attracts, named so they are easier to catch:**

* *Claiming more than the measurement supports.* Asserting that a detection is
  proof, that removal is impossible, or that a verdict means authorship — in an
  issue, a pull request, or a reply — is a conduct problem here, not merely a
  technical one. The tool's honesty is its value, and a contributor who oversells
  it in the project's name damages it.
* *Treating a security report as a joke, or a researcher as an adversary.*
  "Your tool can be defeated" is the expected state of the art for a watermark, and
  [THREAT-MODEL.md](docs/THREAT-MODEL.md) already says it. It is not an insult and
  should not be answered like one.
* *Publicly debugging somebody else's deployment.* A `scan` result about code
  somebody owns is evidence in a private dispute. Do not turn an issue thread into
  the venue for one, and never publish a root secret, a sealed store, or a
  private manifest — the last two are the only things in this project that are
  confidential by default.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour: sexualised language or imagery, personal or
political attacks untethered from the technical content, trolling, insulting or
derogatory comments, public or private harassment, publishing another person's
private information without explicit permission, unpublished security details
obtained under the disclosure process, and other conduct which could reasonably be
considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies in every space of the project: the repository and its
issue and pull-request threads, the chat and mailing-list spaces operated for it,
the sponsor channels, and any conference, meetup or presentation where a person is
speaking as an SWP-1 maintainer or names the project. It also applies to conduct
outside those spaces when it is directed at a contributor's work here and has the
effect of driving them out of the project.

## Reporting

Report to **[contact address from `SECURITY.md`'s maintainer list]**, or to any
maintainer you are comfortable approaching; the list of who that is lives in
[MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md). Reports are read by at least two maintainers
where that is possible, and the identity of a reporter is not shared with the
person reported about without their agreement.

If the person being reported on is a maintainer, you may send the report to a
GitHub organisation owner instead, and that maintainer is excluded from every part
of the handling.

We will not publish a reporter's name, and we will not require a report to be made
under a real name.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki
edits, issues and other contributions that are not aligned to this code of conduct,
and may temporarily or permanently ban any contributor for behaviour they consider
inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

The sequence, where a response is proportionate to the conduct rather than
obvious:

1. **Private, non-public guidance** from a maintainer, with what the problem was.
2. **A public warning** with consequences stated, if the conduct recurs or was
   public.
3. **A temporary ban** from the project's spaces for continued violation.
4. **A permanent ban** where that fails, or immediately for a single serious or
   patterned abuse — doxxing, harassment of an individual, or a deliberate leak of
   somebody's secret material.

Maintainers who do not follow the code of conduct in good faith may be removed
from the maintainer list. We are not above the thing we wrote.

Reported cases and the identities of everyone involved in them are kept
confidential. Where an outcome affects the project as a whole, it may be announced
without identifying anyone, and the reason for that decision will be given.
