# Contributor Licence Agreement — SWP-1

**Version 1.0 · Last revised 2026-09-22**

This agreement is between you and the party defined in §9 as the **Maintainers**.
It says what you permit the Maintainers to do with a contribution you send, and
what you warrant about it. It does **not** transfer your copyright to anyone. You
keep it. What you grant is a licence, and the licence has to be broad enough to
cover uses the Maintainers cannot describe in advance — which is the entire reason
this document exists instead of a two-line waiver.

> **This is not legal advice, and drafting a licence program from a template is a
> decision that should be reviewed by counsel before the first external
> contribution is merged.** In particular, replace the bracketed fields in §9 with
> the legal entity that actually owns this project — "the project's GitHub
> organisation" is not a party that can hold a copyright grant — and confirm that
> §2's relicensing right is expressed the way you need it in your jurisdiction.
> The §9 fields are the same `OWNER` placeholder that appears in `Cargo.toml` and
> `NOTICE`; fill them in once, in all three.

## 1. Definitions

**"Contribution"** means any work of authorship you submit to the SWP-1 project —
code, documentation, test fixtures, language adapters, grammar tables, patches,
review comments you implement yourself, or binaries — intentionally, for inclusion
in the project. A Contribution includes everything you modify *and* everything
your modification depends on that you wrote.

**"Submit"** means to place a Contribution in the Maintainers' reach: a pull
request, an issue attachment, an email, a pushed branch, or a verbal description
you ask to be implemented.

**"the Work"** means the Contribution **and** every derivative of it, including a
derivative produced by combining it with other code, by translating it, by
re-implementing its interface, by statically or dynamically linking it, and by
distributing it as part of a larger program.

**"Distribute"** means to make available, supply, sell, sublicense, host, embed in
a product, or otherwise convey, whether or not for consideration.

project's contribution checklist asks about the origin of an adapter or a grammar
table rather than trusting a "looks clean" read.

You may Submit a later version of something you have already contributed. Unless

## Before you sign

Two things are worth knowing, and neither is hidden on purpose.

**The agreement favours the project's side.** A Contributor Licence Agreement
exists because it gives one party rights the other does not get. Contributor
Covenant — what `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` governs — is about how people treat each
other; this document is about who may ship what. Contributors who want a project
whose licence cannot change usually contribute to copyleft projects instead, and
that is a reasonable thing to want.

**A DCO is the alternative, and it is weaker.** A
[Developer Certificate of Origin](https://developercertificate.org/) sign-off
states that you *may* submit the code; it grants nothing to the project beyond
the licence the code already carries. That is enough for a project that will stay
under one licence forever, and it is the fallback this project would use if it
gave up the rights in §2 — see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#the-cla-and-the-dco).
It is not enough for a project that intends to build a separate commercial
product on the same code, which is what §2 is for.
